Hickory Sands District AVA

A proposed American Viticultural Area

The Hickory Sands District AVA is a proposed winegrowing district in the Texas Hill Country, drawn on a single geological idea: vineyard land that sits on the Hickory Sandstone Member, at 1,500 feet elevation or higher, directly over the Hickory aquifer. Where the sandstone gives way to granite, the boundary stops. The granite was left out on purpose.

It would be the first Texas AVA designed from the outset to sit entirely within two other AVAs at once, the Texas Hill Country AVA and the proposed Llano Uplift AVA. The petition was filed by the growers who farm this land, and the federal rulemaking is under way now.

The land

The district is defined by the Hickory Sandstone Member of the Hickory formation. Rain that falls on the sandstone filters down into the Hickory aquifer, and the wells out here draw that water back up for irrigation. The sandy Keese and Ligon soils over the sandstone drain fast and hold little back, which is hard on vines in exactly the way that makes good wine. Outside the boundary, the Llano Uplift granite takes over, and the farming changes with it.

The growers

The petitioners are Robert Clay Vineyards, Parr Vineyards, and Tallent Vineyards. Vineyard owners across the four counties support the district, farmers who also happen to be making some of the most highly scored wine in Texas.
