# PUBLIC COMMENT

**Docket No. TTB-2026-0005, Notice No. 243: Proposed Establishment of the Llano Uplift and Hickory Sands District Viticultural Areas**

Submitted by: Dan McLaughlin, Proprietor and Vigneron, Robert Clay Vineyards, Mason, Texas

Date: October 13, 2026

## I. Introduction and Summary

I am a co-petitioner for the proposed Hickory Sands District AVA, together with Parr Vineyards and Tallent Vineyards. I drew the AVA boundary map submitted with our petition and wrote the petition's 102-step written boundary description. I strongly support establishment of the Hickory Sands District AVA, and nothing in this comment is intended to delay it. None of the requests below is a condition of my support, and each can be addressed in the final rule.

TTB invited comments on the sufficiency and accuracy of the boundary (Notice No. 243, Comments Invited). This comment responds to that invitation.

I respectfully request three things in the final rule. The first is the most important:

1. **Restore the petition's routing in the western section of the boundary.** The proposed text differs from the petition's routing near Grit, Texas. The difference adds approximately 4.3 square miles of exposed granite that the petition deliberately excluded, and it is the reason the proposed text describes about 198 square miles rather than the 193 square miles stated in the notice. Restoring the petition routing would bring the boundary description back in line with the notice's own area figure and with the evidence on which the AVA is based.
2. **Correct the published GIS polygon so that it follows the written boundary description.** Apart from the Grit section, the published polygon departs from the proposed text in a number of places totaling about 790 acres. These are technical corrections.
3. **Make several minor wording corrections** so that each step of the description matches the designated USGS maps.

Unless stated otherwise, step and point numbers below refer to the proposed text. A point number refers to the end of the step with the same number.

## II. How the Boundary Was Checked

To compare the versions of the boundary on equal terms, each step of the proposed text was drawn on the 13 designated USGS 1:24,000 quadrangles. Each point was placed on the printed mark or intersection the text names, and each line follows the printed feature the text names (road centerline, creek centerline, or contour line). The petition routing was redrawn on the same maps in the same way. All areas below were measured on the GRS 1980 ellipsoid in NAD83, the datum of TTB's AVA layer.

| Boundary | Area |
|---|---|
| Proposed text, drawn on the USGS maps | 198.1 sq mi |
| Proposed text with the petition's western routing restored, drawn on the same maps | 193.8 sq mi |
| TTB published polygon (AVAs_Production, Hickory Sands District, retrieved October 10, 2026) | 197.6 sq mi |

The notice describes a 193-square-mile AVA in its summary and in its conclusions. The difference between the first two rows is the Grit section described in Section III.

## III. The Grit Section

The difference between the petition and the proposed text is essentially one piece of land: a section of approximately 4.32 square miles (2,767 acres) around Grit, between State Highway 29 and U.S. Highway 377. It enters the AVA at the western section of the boundary.

- **Petition steps 91 through 98:** follow Honey Creek to the marked 1,727-foot elevation point at State Highway 29; proceed northwesterly along the eastbound lane of State Highway 29 past U.S. Highway 377 to the marked 1,737-foot elevation point; proceed southwest to the marked 1,762-foot elevation point and to the end of an unnamed road; follow that road to U.S. Highway 377; proceed southeast to the marked 1,765-foot summit; then southeast to the end of Lange Road.
- **Proposed steps 90 through 92:** follow Honey Creek to its intersection with U.S. Highway 377 at the marked 1,668-foot elevation point; follow U.S. Highway 377 southwest, then northwest, to Old Junction Road; then southeast to the terminus of Lange Road.

The petition route stays north of the granite, along State Highway 29. The proposed route runs south of it, along Honey Creek and U.S. Highway 377, and so takes the granite section into the AVA. The proposed route also bypasses the petition's 1,727-foot point, 1,762-foot point and 1,765-foot summit. The published polygon follows the proposed text here; the Grit section is a change in the boundary description itself.

This change affects the area the petition documented, and I ask that TTB give it careful consideration. The notice does not mention the change: it refers readers to the proposed regulatory text for the petitioned-for boundary and describes a 193-square-mile area. I first raised the Grit section with TTB staff as a difference between the published polygon and the petition. After drawing the proposed text step by step on the designated maps, I found that the published polygon follows the proposed text here, and that the difference comes from the routing in proposed steps 90 through 92. If the change was made because a petition step was difficult to locate on the maps, I would be glad to help resolve that: each of the petition's points in this section is printed on the designated quadrangles. Replacement text for the proposed steps is provided in Section VIII.

## IV. Why the Petition Routes Around the Grit Section

The Grit section is exposed Precambrian granite. On the USGS State Geologic Map Compilation for Texas, much of it is mapped as younger granitic intrusion. Texas Water Development Board groundwater records show no irrigation wells within the section; the nearest recorded irrigation well lies about 0.15 miles from its edge.

Much of the section is used for commercial granite rock-crawling parks, whose trail networks are visible on current aerial imagery (Exhibit B), appear on the parks' published trail maps, and are mapped in OpenStreetMap (Exhibit A):

| Park | Approximate area inside the Grit section | Advertised size |
|---|---|---|
| Katemcy Rocks (K2), off State Highway 29 | about 750 acres | about 800 acres |
| Wolf Caves, U.S. Highway 377 | about 477 acres | 430 acres |
| **Total** | **about 1,227 acres (44% of the section)** | |

The mapped areas were interpreted from aerial imagery and the parks' trail maps and are limited to land north of U.S. Highway 377; they are approximate, not surveyed.

The petition establishes the AVA on coarse, sandy soils primarily of the Keese and Ligon series, formed from eroded Precambrian bedrock and Cambrian sedimentary rocks including the Hickory Sandstone; on low fertility and low salinity; on silicate sand inhospitable to phylloxera; and on groundwater from the Hickory Aquifer. The Grit section lacks these soil and groundwater characteristics. As the petitioner who drew this boundary to follow plantable ground, I routed around this section because it has little soil cover and no demonstrated irrigation supply.

The petition draws this distinction itself. It explains that the Hickory Sands District follows the Hickory Sandstone Member, and it describes the granite outcroppings of the Llano Uplift as rock that can "intrude upon the surface, preventing adequate soil cover." It acknowledges that the USGS map constraints required including small areas not situated directly on the Hickory Sandstone, while stating that "all efforts were made to minimize the inclusion of such acreage." The petition routing along both sides of State Highway 29 is one of those efforts. The Grit section belongs with the granite core of the Llano Uplift rather than with the sandstone district.

Mapped geology shows how different the Grit section is from the rest of the AVA. On the USGS State Geologic Map Compilation for Texas, the geologic source cited in the petition, the granite units (Town Mountain Granite and younger granitic intrusions) cover approximately:

| Area | Acres | Mapped granite (approximate) | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grit section | 2,767 | about 2,400 acres | about 87% |
| Within the petition boundary | 124,024 | about 2,800 acres | about 2.3% |
| Within the proposed text | 126,767 | about 5,200 acres | about 4% |

The compilation is generalized, so these figures are approximate. Most of the granite within the petition boundary lies in scattered pieces within about 500 meters of boundary segments that follow roads, streams, or lines between marked points on the designated USGS maps; in my judgment as the petitioner, it could not be excluded with features on those maps without also excluding Hickory Sandstone ground. The Grit section is a single area that features on the designated maps allow to be excluded, and the petition excluded it. At quadrangle scale, the Bureau of Economic Geology's 1:24,000 geologic map of the Grit quadrangle (Hunt, Johnson, and Helper, 2021, Open-File Map 252) maps the Grit section predominantly as Precambrian granite, Streeter Granite and Town Mountain Granite, with alluvium and terrace deposits along the creeks and a small area of Packsaddle Schist at its northern tip. To the west, the petition routing follows the nearest features printed on the designated maps along the granite's western margin, leaving a fringe of granite inside the boundary, and the land beyond it is mapped as Hickory Sandstone Member (Exhibit D).

## V. The Lane Wording

Petition steps 11 and 12 route the boundary along the "westbound lane" of State Highway 29 and U.S. Highway 377, and petition steps 92 and 93 along the "eastbound lane" of State Highway 29. Following the highways out along one side and back along the other is how the petition excludes the granite between them. The proposed text omits the lane wording in steps 10 and 11, and the eastbound-lane steps were removed with the western change.

Before the petition was submitted, TTB staff advised that the boundary follow features on the USGS topographic maps and enclose a single contiguous area. To meet that guidance while keeping the granite near Grit outside the AVA, I routed the petition boundary along the lanes of State Highway 29. I recognize that, written this way, the petition routing follows the same stretch of State Highway 29 in both directions, and that the USGS maps do not show the highway's lanes separately. At least one established AVA is drawn this way. In TTB's own AVA layer (AVAs_Production, retrieved October 12, 2026), the San Pasqual Valley boundary (27 CFR 9.25(c)(2)) goes out along a single line of about 380 meters to leave a hillock of about 220 acres outside the AVA, and returns along the same line (Exhibit C, and the San Pasqual Valley feature in Attachment 2, unaltered). TTB may consider that approach if useful. I defer entirely to TTB on the wording. What matters to the petition is that the final description keep the section between State Highway 29 and U.S. Highway 377 near Grit outside the AVA, in any form TTB finds workable.

## VI. The Published Polygon

Separately from the Grit section, I compared TTB's published polygon with the proposed text drawn on the USGS maps. These are technical issues with the map, not with the boundary description, and I offer them to help TTB publish a map that matches the final rule. The published polygon departs from the proposed text in 69 places of 2 acres or more, totaling about 790 acres: about 540 acres the text includes but the polygon leaves out, and about 250 acres the polygon includes but the text leaves out. Each place was reviewed on the map scans. By cause:

| Cause | Places | Acres |
|---|---|---|
| Polygon corner placed away from the printed mark or intersection the text names | 31 | about 571 |
| Polygon cuts across contour lobes and road or creek bends the text follows | 33 | about 131 |
| Polygon omits a point the text names | 1 | about 56 |
| Polygon follows a different feature than the text names | 4 | about 34 |

The largest:

- **Point 16 (about 140 acres):** the polygon's corner is about 163 m east of the marked 1,653-foot elevation point on Thomas Road, so the lines to and from it swing east and leave out land the text includes.
- **Point 23 (about 56 acres):** the polygon goes directly from point 22 to point 24 and does not reach the marked 1,625-foot elevation point named in step 23.
- **Points 31 through 35 (about 36 acres):** each corner is 9 to 45 m from its printed mark.
- **Points 50 through 52 (about 30 acres)** and **point 29 (about 27 acres):** corners away from the marks; at point 29 the corner is about 105 m from the creek fork the text names.
- **Step 9 (about 34 acres in all):** the text says "southerly along Honey Creek," but for about 1 km the polygon follows an unpaved road east of the creek.

About 80 of the 790 acres involve marks that can reasonably be read more than one way, and I have identified them as such: points 92 and 93 (the end of Lange Road and the Big Bluff Creek crossing), point 76 (the Ellison Cemetery, printed about 19 m west of Brockman Lane), point 19 and point 46. For these I note the reading I used and defer to TTB.

The complete list, with the step, cause and area of each place, is provided as a GIS layer (Attachment 2). The point and line layers in Attachment 2 may be used to redraw the polygon if helpful.

## VII. Minor Wording Corrections

These can be made in the final rule. Several carry over from the petition's own wording, and I ask that they be corrected in the final text:

1. **Step 4:** "the marked 1,866-foot elevation point." The Long Mountain map prints both a benchmark, BM 1866, on State Highway 29 near Ranch Road 1222, and a separate spot elevation of 1866 about 1.2 km east. The intended point is the benchmark. Suggested wording: "to benchmark 1,866." (The published polygon's corner is about 37 m east of the benchmark, which makes step 5 run slightly west of north rather than northeast; placing the corner on the benchmark resolves this.)
2. **Step 44:** "benchmark 1,495." The Voca map prints BR 1495, a bridge elevation, with no benchmark symbol. Suggested wording: "to the marked 1,495-foot elevation point at the bridge on."
3. **Step 46:** "the intersection of two unnamed roads known locally as Private Road 626 and Private Road 669." On the Voca map these roads join the east-west road at two junctions about 54 m apart. Suggested wording: identify the eastern junction, directly under the printed 1554, which is the petition point.
4. **Step 49:** "the marked 1,586-elevation point" should read "the marked 1,586-foot elevation point."
5. **Steps 80 and 81:** the 1967 Mason map prints no road south of State Highway 29 at the Old Pontotoc Road intersection, only a land line; the nearest printed road leaves State Highway 29 about 285 m west. Please confirm that step 81 follows the line due south from the intersection, as the petition intended.
6. **Step 84:** "the marked 1,610-foot summit." The 1610 mark is printed in a drainage. Suggested wording: "the marked 1,610-foot elevation point."
7. **Step 85:** "the marked 1,801-foot summit." The Turtle Creek map prints 1802 at this summit.
8. **Steps 90 and 91:** "State Highway 377" should read "U.S. Highway 377" (if these steps remain).

## VIII. Requested Action

1. **Western section:** to conform the regulatory text to the petition TTB evaluated and to the 193-square-mile area the notice describes, retain proposed step 89 ("west in a straight line to Honey Creek") and replace proposed steps 90 through 92 with the petition's steps 91 through 98 (petition steps 92 and 93 combine into new step 91 below), renumbering the remaining steps. For TTB's convenience, suggested replacement text in the style of the proposed rule (map-crossing notes to be confirmed by TTB):

   (90) Proceed northeasterly, then northwesterly, then northeasterly along Honey Creek, crossing onto the Grit map, to the creek's intersection with State Highway 29 at the marked 1,727-foot elevation point; then

   (91) Proceed northwesterly along the eastbound lane of State Highway 29, past its intersection with U.S. Highway 377, to the marked 1,737-foot elevation point; then

   (92) Proceed southwest in a straight line to the marked 1,762-foot elevation point; then

   (93) Proceed southwest in a straight line to the end of an unnamed, unimproved road; then

   (94) Proceed southwesterly along the unnamed road to its intersection with U.S. Highway 377; then

   (95) Proceed southeast in a straight line to the marked 1,765-foot summit; then

   (96) Proceed southeast in a straight line, crossing onto the Turtle Creek map, to the terminus of an unnamed, unimproved road known locally as Lange Road; then

   These steps follow the petition's routing. TTB may adopt them as written or reword them; they are offered to show the intended line. The petition's lane wording in proposed steps 10 and 11 is one way to keep the granite section outside the AVA (see Section V); any wording TTB finds workable that achieves the same exclusion would be equally welcome.

2. **Published polygon:** redraw it to follow the final written boundary description, correcting the departures identified in Section VI.
3. **Wording:** make the corrections listed in Section VII.
4. If TTB had a reason for the western change that the petitioners are not aware of, I would welcome the opportunity to discuss it with TTB staff.
5. If TTB concludes that the western routing should remain as proposed, I nonetheless support establishment of the AVA as proposed and ask TTB to proceed without delay.

I support the Hickory Sands District AVA and ask TTB to proceed with establishing it. I ask only that the final boundary match the area the petition documented and that the published map match the final text. I am glad to provide the GIS data and to assist TTB staff with any of these corrections.
